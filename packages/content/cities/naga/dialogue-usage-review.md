# Naga dialogue: local usage review

Reviewed on 2026-10-02 after the owner's corrections to unfamiliar wording. All 100 exchanges were reread; 20 exchanges (25 turns) were revised in this pass. There are still 100 exchanges and 199 turns. Selection metadata, speaker roles, event timing and category allocation are unchanged.

The result is mixed: several core forms have identifiable Naga/local usage, while the complete exchanges remain authored fiction. No recorded conversation corpus or independent Naga speaker has validated the entire catalog. A locally published word, a grammatical sentence and a natural spoken exchange are different kinds of evidence. This review does not assign a misleading percentage of "native-approved" lines.

Loanwords and ordinary code-switching are retained when they serve a brief conversation: merienda, praktis, fountain, monumento, po, pasensya and pakiulit. They are not automatically errors because another language also uses them. No AI translation site is used as evidence of local authenticity. Nearby regional varieties and translated religious prose are not treated as proof of a uniquely Naga conversational preference.

## Findings and corrections

- Apply the owner's halat correction to the remaining waiting forms: halat, mahalat, maghalat and naghahalat. Remove hulatan rather than inventing a replacement noun without evidence.
- Use shorter state expressions for hunger and tiredness: gutom and pagal. This is an editorial choice supported by the roots; it is not a claim that every longer alternative is impossible or never spoken.
- Use maghiling when the speaker intends to look around. Mahiling as a future form can be grammatical in other contexts; these stall lines now make the intended act clearer.
- State the passing request in courtesy-excuse with maki-agi. Retain po for this polite Naga-oriented scene.
- Simplify the emotional reunion line and the rain-easing remark. Preserve the translations' intent and the actual scene conditions.
- Keep legitimate reciprocal greetings, quantity confirmation and thanks. Repetition is appropriate when it performs a conversational function.
